## 2. The burden this addresses, from an operator's chair

The regulatory load on a provider our size is not a background cost — it is a job.
Between electronic visit verification, the compensation requirements in CMS's 2024
access rule, critical-incident reporting obligations, and the waiver rules that
govern who we may serve and how, my staff and I spend real working hours trying to
determine what a rule actually requires. That is time not spent with the people in
our care.

The people who most need those answers are the least equipped to go find them. An
aide asking whether a particular visit needs verification, or what has to be
reported and how quickly, is not a compliance lawyer and should not have to be one.
When we get it wrong, the consequence is not a bad grade — it is a failed audit, a
recoupment, or a finding that follows us.

I have reviewed the pr4xis HCBS/EVV Compliance Navigator. In plain terms:

- It answers a regulatory question in plain language and **shows the actual federal
  statute or regulation the answer came from**, so my staff can check it rather
  than trust it.
- When it does not hold a cited answer, **it declines and says what is missing**
  instead of producing a confident guess. For compliance work this is the whole
  point: an answer that sounds authoritative and is wrong is worse than no answer,
  because nobody thinks to check it.
- It is careful about the boundary between federal rules and state or vendor
  policy — where the governing rule is our state's, it says so rather than
  answering from federal law as though the two were the same. That distinction is
  exactly where I have seen people get into trouble.
- **Every query produces a record of the question, the answer, and the rule behind
  it**, which we could retain under our own governance as part of an audit trail.
- It runs in a browser with no account and no per-query cost, and questions do not
  leave the device — so no vendor receives our staff's compliance questions.

I am not representing it as finished, and its authors have not claimed that to me.
I am supporting it because the failure mode it is built to avoid is the one that
actually costs providers like us.
