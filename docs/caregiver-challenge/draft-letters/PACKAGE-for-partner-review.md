## What the tool actually is

Plain version, no jargon.

It's a free web page. A family caregiver — or one of your staff — types a question
in ordinary language. For example: *"Is respite care the same as adult day care?"*
or *"Does this visit need electronic verification?"*

Three things make it different from the AI tools your families and staff are
already using:

**1. Every answer shows its source.** Not a summary of a source — the actual
statute, regulation, or official federal guidance the answer came from, with the
citation, so the person can check it instead of trusting it.

**2. When it doesn't know, it says so.** This is the design decision at the centre
of the whole thing. If it does not hold a reliable, cited answer, it declines and
points to a person. It does not produce a confident-sounding guess. General-purpose
AI tools do the opposite — they will answer anything, fluently, whether or not they
have grounds to. In caregiving that's dangerous: a family that acts on a wrong
answer about an enrollment window can lose services for months, and nobody thinks
to double-check an answer that sounded authoritative.

**3. Nothing leaves the device.** It runs inside the person's own browser. No
account, no sign-in, no question sent to any company's servers — which matters when
somebody is asking about a diagnosis or a family member's capacity. There's also no
per-use cost and no server to run, so an organization can put it in front of
families without a budget line or an IT project.

## What it does not do yet — so you know what you'd be supporting

I'd rather you hear this from me than discover it later.

- **Nobody has used it in a real care setting.** There has been no usability testing
  with families or care workers. That is precisely what I'm asking your help with.
- **It answers a limited set of questions today.** It's grounded in federal law and
  program guidance, so questions governed by state policy or a specific vendor's
  rules will usually get a decline rather than an answer.
- **It's a working demonstration, not a product.** There's no support line, no
  service agreement, no uptime commitment.
- **It gets things wrong.** We keep a public log of its own failures, and we submit
  that log with the application rather than only the parts that work.

I'm not asking you to endorse it as finished. I'm asking you to say that the
approach — refuse rather than guess, and always show the source — is the right one
for the people you serve, and that you're willing to help test it against reality.
