# Bus ontology — bibliography

## Primary sources

- **Eugster, P. T., Felber, P. A., Guerraoui, R. & Kermarrec, A.-M. (2003).** *"The Many Faces of Publish/Subscribe"*. ACM Computing Surveys 35(2), 114–131. DOI: [10.1145/857076.857078](https://doi.org/10.1145/857076.857078). Grounds `MessageBus` (§1), `Broker` (§2), `Event`, `Topic` (§4.1), `Publisher`, `Subscriber`, `TopicBasedRouting` (§4.1), `Decoupling` (§2 — space, time, and synchronization decoupling), the `Publishes`/`Subscribes`/`Decouples` edges, the `RoutingStrategy` and `IsSpaceDecoupled` qualities, and the `PubSubDecoupling` axiom (the deliberate absence of any `Publisher`→`Subscriber` edge).
- **Carzaniga, A., Rosenblum, D. S. & Wolf, A. L. (2001).** *"Design and Evaluation of a Wide-Area Event Notification Service"*. ACM Transactions on Computer Systems 19(3), 332–383 (SIENA). DOI: [10.1145/380749.380767](https://doi.org/10.1145/380749.380767). Grounds `Subscription` (the registered interest as a predicate over notifications), `ContentBasedRouting`, the `Routes` and `Matches` edges, and the `RoutingDichotomy` axiom.
- **Hewitt, C., Bishop, P. & Steiger, R. (1973).** *"A Universal Modular ACTOR Formalism for Artificial Intelligence"*. IJCAI '73, 235–245. Grounds `Message` (the self-contained payload as the sole unit of communication), `Actor`, the `ActorMessagesOnly` axiom (an actor communicates solely by messages), and — with Agha (1986) — the point-to-point (`IsSpaceDecoupled = false`) classification of the actor pair.
- **Agha, G. A. (1986).** *Actors: A Model of Concurrent Computation in Distributed Systems*. The MIT Press (MIT Press Series in Artificial Intelligence). ISBN 0-262-01092-5. Grounds `Mailbox` (the actor's mail queue at its mail address — the mailbox terminology absent from Hewitt et al. 1973) and the `Actor` has-a `Mailbox` Parthood edge.
- **Spector, A. Z. (1982).** *"Performing Remote Operations Efficiently on a Local Computer Network"*. Communications of the ACM 25(4), 246–260. DOI: [10.1145/358468.358478](https://doi.org/10.1145/358468.358478). Grounds (with Birrell & Nelson 1984 and MQTT 5.0 §4.3) the delivery-semantics trichotomy — `DeliveryGuarantee`, `AtMostOnce` (fire-and-forget), `AtLeastOnce`, `ExactlyOnce` — the `DeliverySemantics` quality, and the `ThreeDeliveryGuarantees` and `DeliverySemanticsBehavioral` axioms.
- **Birrell, A. D. & Nelson, B. J. (1984).** *"Implementing Remote Procedure Calls"*. ACM Transactions on Computer Systems 2(1), 39–59. DOI: [10.1145/2080.357392](https://doi.org/10.1145/2080.357392). Grounds the retransmission / lost-acknowledgment / duplicate-suppression mechanics behind `AtLeastOnce`, the engine's `Transmission`/`Retry` protocol, and the fixture's `UNACKED_MESSAGE`.
- **OASIS (2019).** *MQTT Version 5.0*, OASIS Standard, §4.3 "Quality of Service levels and protocol flows" (QoS 0 = at most once, QoS 1 = at least once, QoS 2 = exactly once). [docs.oasis-open.org/mqtt/mqtt/v5.0](https://docs.oasis-open.org/mqtt/mqtt/v5.0/mqtt-v5.0.html). The modern pub/sub statement of the delivery-semantics trichotomy.
- **Cristian, F. (1991).** *"Understanding Fault-Tolerant Distributed Systems"*. Communications of the ACM 34(2), 56–78. DOI: [10.1145/102792.102801](https://doi.org/10.1145/102792.102801). Grounds the engine's omission-failure model (the channel may drop a message or its acknowledgment). See also **Hadzilacos, V. & Toueg, S. (1993)**, *"Fault-Tolerant Broadcasts and Related Problems"*, in S. Mullender (ed.), *Distributed Systems* (2nd ed.), Addison-Wesley — the modular fault-model treatment.
- **Birman, K. P. & Joseph, T. A. (1987).** *"Reliable Communication in the Presence of Failures"*. ACM Transactions on Computer Systems 5(1), 47–76 (ISIS). DOI: [10.1145/7351.7478](https://doi.org/10.1145/7351.7478). Grounds the atomic all-or-nothing reading of `ExactlyOnce` (delivered exactly once at every operational destination), the underlying broadcast primitives behind `VirtualSynchrony`, and the `Delivers` edge. (The at-most-once / at-least-once / exactly-once *terminology* is not from this paper — see Spector 1982, Birrell & Nelson 1984, MQTT 5.0 §4.3 above.)
- **Birman, K. P. & Joseph, T. A. (1987).** *"Exploiting Virtual Synchrony in Distributed Systems"*. SOSP '87 / ACM SIGOPS Operating Systems Review 21(5), 123–138. DOI: [10.1145/37499.37515](https://doi.org/10.1145/37499.37515). Grounds `VirtualSynchrony` (the term and its definition: ordered, gap-free multicast within a view).

## Functor source

- **von Bertalanffy, L. (1968).** *General System Theory: Foundations, Development, Applications*. George Braziller, New York. Grounds the `Bus → System` functor: components, interactions, boundaries, constraints, homeostasis.

## Honest-tier notes

- **`ExactlyOnce` is glossed as contested.** End-to-end exactly-once delivery over a lossy channel is not achievable by the transport alone: the sender cannot distinguish a lost message from a lost acknowledgment, so any policy that recovers loss can duplicate. What *is* achievable — and what the gloss, the engine, and the `DeliverySemanticsBehavioral` axiom demonstrate — is at-least-once transport plus transactional/deduplicating cooperation of the endpoints. The axiom is grounded in Birman & Joseph's (1987) virtual-synchrony delivery ("delivered exactly once at every operational destination"), which is a *system-level* guarantee built from exactly such cooperation, not a bare-channel property.
- **The engine is a simulator, not a protocol implementation.** The broker fixture demonstrates the three semantics' observable contracts (loss / duplication / exact hand-off) on a canonical failure trace; it does not implement ISIS's ABCAST/CBCAST protocols.
- **Trichotomy regrounding (LLM-checked tier).** The at-most-once / at-least-once / exactly-once trichotomy was previously mis-attributed to Birman & Joseph (1987) TOCS 5(1), where those terms do not appear. It is regrounded to the RPC call-semantics lineage (Spector 1982; Birrell & Nelson 1984) and to its modern pub/sub statement (OASIS MQTT 5.0 §4.3, QoS 0/1/2); the omission-failure model to Cristian (1991); `Mailbox` to Agha (1986); and `VirtualSynchrony` to Birman & Joseph's (1987) SOSP paper. Author/year/venue/DOIs and the §4.3 QoS mapping were web-verified (LLM-checked tier, not machine-verified against a loaded corpus).
